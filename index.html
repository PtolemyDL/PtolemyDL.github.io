04/ HOW I WORK

FOUR STEPS.
NO THREE-MONTH BRANCHES.

Hardware punishes hand-waving.

I want the load path, failure mode, and physical principle behind a decision. Rules of thumb are useful shortcuts only after the mechanism is understood.

A quick fixture exposes assumptions that a perfect CAD model cannot. I build early, define measurable test conditions, and push until the weak link becomes visible.

DFM/A compounds. Every revision is a chance to simplify assembly, control tolerance risk, reduce part count, and move the design closer to repeatable production.

Clear requirements, decision records, test methods, and results keep learning from evaporating. Good documentation makes the next iteration faster and safer.

HANDHELD
SEMG DEVICE

Handheld surface-EMG device for physiotherapy: 1 kHz sampling, real-time filtering, touchscreen UI.

ROLE
Biomedical Engineering Co-op — Embedded Systems & Signal Processing Lead
TIMELINE
May–August 2024
CONTEXT
The KITE Research Institute (Toronto Rehab)
STATUS
Functional prototype validated.

01OVERVIEW

Redesigning an early-stage handheld surface-EMG prototype into a clinically viable tool for physiotherapists and occupational therapists — real-time muscle-activity monitoring with better signal quality, usability and system performance.

02DESIGN OBJECTIVES

  • Accurate real-time sEMG visualization in a compact handheld form.
  • Better signal quality via digital filtering and optimized data handling.
  • Hardware/firmware redesign for efficient processing and clinician-friendly operation.

03KEY FEATURES

Hardware integration

Nextion 3.5" touchscreen offloading graphics, stable 1 kHz sampling, redesigned enclosure with physical controls and electrode ports.

Signal processing & firmware

Digital bandpass + notch filters, real-time acquisition alongside touch UI, simple gesture logic (tap-left pause, tap-right return).

Prototyping & validation

Tested with wet and dry electrodes, with high-fidelity captures from biceps and forearm flexors.

02 / 05CASE STUDY

PHANES

Passive exoskeleton for spinal-cord-injury mobility: sit-to-stand plus elliptical training, no motors.

ROLE
R&D Lead & Biomechanical Engineer
TIMELINE
2021–2023
CONTEXT
Team of 5 mechanical engineers (initial design solo, later co-developed)
STATUS
Prototype transferred to fabrication partners.

01OVERVIEW

PHANES is a mobility-enhancing rehabilitation device for people living with spinal cord injuries — a sit-to-stand mechanism combined with a modified elliptical system, so users can transition independently from sitting to standing and do guided exercise at home. Non-powered and affordable by design.

02DESIGN OBJECTIVES

  • Access to physical activity for SCI users through affordable non-powered design.
  • Independent sit↔stand transitions.
  • Modified elliptical for upper/lower-body coordination.
  • Accessibility, safety and manufacturability.

03KEY FEATURES

Sit-to-stand

Hand-powered mechanism built around the user's functional upper-body strength, 5 design iterations, FEA-validated, user-centered motion analysis.

Elliptical

Commercial trainer modified for coordinated motion, linkage geometry from MATLAB biomechanical modeling, custom push-pull test rig.

Accessibility

Integrated transfer system, modular seat and harness.

$25K R&D funding raised via pitch competitions · 26+ engineering drawings · fabrication with AHBM Systems & EMS E3.

04 / 05CASE STUDY

PASSIVE KNEE
ROM DEVICE

Non-motorized knee rehab device from a 48-hour challenge: pneumatic cushions drive 180°→130° flexion.

ROLE
Lead Mechanical Concept & Biomechanics Designer
TIMELINE
48-hour design challenge
CONTEXT
Hosted by Jack McDonald — Biomedical Device Innovation Challenge
STATUS
Preliminary concept.

01OVERVIEW

A non-motorized rehabilitation concept for post-knee-replacement patients, designed in 48 hours: pneumatic cushions and an ergonomic orthotic structure assist safe, independent knee flexion-extension at home — no supervision, no electrical power.

02DESIGN OBJECTIVES

  • Passive flexion-extension from 180° to 130° simulating CPM therapy.
  • Zero powered actuation (Class I device target).
  • Ergonomic comfort for extended relaxed use.
  • Modular lightweight components.
  • Mechanical hard stops for safe user-adjustable motion.

03KEY FEATURES

Mechanism

Four angled air bladders (2 under thigh, 2 above shank) acting as linear actuators, manual pump with valve isolation per segment, hard-stop hinge with mechanical lock at 130°.

Structure

Lightweight polycarbonate frame, medical-grade TPU cushions, foam liners and Velcro harnesses.

Analysis

Full-body kinematic/kinetic simulation on 75th-percentile anthropometric data, passive joint resistance and actuator force profiling, engineering spec tables from biomechanical analysis.

05 / 05CASE STUDY

PICK-AND-PLACE

6DOF robotic pick-and-place with a satellite gear wrist: one motor drives grip and rotation.

ROLE
Mechanical Designer
TIMELINE
2023
CONTEXT
University of Waterloo — ME380 Mechanical Design
STATUS
Designed, prototyped, tested.

01OVERVIEW

A six-degrees-of-freedom pick-and-place mechanism with a compact satellite gear system driving both grip and wrist rotation — mechanically efficient, manufacturable, and compact for robotic manipulation tasks.

02DESIGN OBJECTIVES

  • 6DOF precise pick-and-place in a defined workspace.
  • Gear-driven wrist + gripper via satellite configuration.
  • Fabrication with accessible tools (3D printing, laser cutting).

03KEY FEATURES

DOF integration

Stacked joints + linear rails combining translation and rotation, with wrist and grip synchronized through one mechanical input.

Satellite gear system

Compact planetary set in the gripper: one motor controls grip and rotation simultaneously, with less wiring and higher reliability.

Mechanical design

Fully modeled and kinematically simulated in SolidWorks, with parts optimized for PLA printing and laser-cut structures.

Full CAD with exploded views · fabrication drawings + BOM · demo videos.

LEARNING NOTE / Deepened my understanding of motion coupling — mechanical simplicity wins in multi-DOF systems.
